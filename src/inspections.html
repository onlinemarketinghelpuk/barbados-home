Shipping Act 1994 · sections 202 to 208

Flag State inspections

All Barbadian vessels must be inspected annually. Inspections are carried out by our worldwide network of Appointed Nautical Inspectors.

Find an inspector Types of inspection

Required by law

Every year

All Barbadian vessels must be inspected annually, under sections 202 to 208 of the Shipping Act 1994.

Appointed Nautical Inspectors

Worldwide

Our ANIs are not restricted to a region, so they can inspect a vessel in any location.

After the inspection

On display

A Certificate of Inspection is issued, and must be displayed in a prominent place on board, typically in the accommodation or on the bridge.

Arranging an inspection

Vessel owners or managers arrange the annual inspection directly with one of our ANIs.

Types of inspection

Five inspections, one standard

The BMSR and our ANIs carry out several types of inspection over a ship's time on the register.

  1. 1

    Pre-registration inspection

    Ships over 12 years old

    Required for all ships over 12 years of age proposed for registration, to assess their technical suitability for provisional registration. This requirement may be waived at the Principal Registrar's discretion.

  2. 2

    Initial inspection

    Within provisional registration

    Carried out within the period of provisional registration (normally six months). A vessel cannot be permanently registered before the initial inspection has been carried out.

  3. 3

    Annual Flag Safety Inspection (AFSI)

    ± 3 months of the anniversary date

    Confirms that the ship maintains the registration, operational safety and pollution prevention standards required by the BMSR, and remains technically suitable for continued registration. Conducted within three months either side of the vessel's anniversary date, as shown on the Certificate of Registry.

  4. 4

    Follow-up inspection

    After a port State control inspection

    May be carried out where the vessel incurs more than three deficiencies, or is detained, during a Port State Control Inspection (PSCI). It verifies that all deficiencies have been properly rectified, and that measures are in place to prevent them recurring.

  5. 5

    Additional inspection

    One-off, requested by the BMSR

    A one-off inspection that may be requested by the BMSR.

    Possible reasons
    • Persistent failure to rectify deficiencies by the due dates, or recurrence of deficiencies
    • Port State control inspections (PSCIs) and AFSIs
    • Class and statutory surveys
    • International Safety Management (ISM) Code audits
    • IACS Procedural Requirement PR17: reporting on deficiencies possibly affecting the implementation of the ISM Code on board
    • Information on serious defects or substandard conditions received from BMSR inspectors, Recognised Organisation surveyors and auditors, other Administrations, etc.
    • Information received from BMSR ANIs, port State control officers, RO surveyors, crew members, etc., of serious breaches of the Maritime Labour Convention, 2006
